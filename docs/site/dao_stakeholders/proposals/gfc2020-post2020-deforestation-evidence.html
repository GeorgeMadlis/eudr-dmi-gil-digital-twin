Add dynamically versioned GFC2020-based post-2020 deforestation evidence layer to the EUDR Digital Twin

Proposal ID: SCP-2026-001-gfc2020-post2020-deforestation-evidence | Status: DRAFT | Created: 2026-07-04

Problem Statement

The current Digital Twin needs a clear, inspectable method for estimating whether post-2020 forest disturbance occurred inside land that was forest at the EUDR cut-off date. GFC2020 provides a harmonised 2020 forest baseline, but by itself it does not detect post-2020 disturbance or prove agricultural conversion. A new evidence layer is needed to combine GFC2020 with post-2020 change datasets while preserving evidence gaps and dataset conflicts. Because source datasets are updated over time, the evidence period must be resolved dynamically from dataset availability rather than hard-coded.

Non-decision rule: this proposal does not assert EUDR compliance or non-compliance.

DAO (Stakeholders) Questions

  1. Should JRC GFC2020 be accepted as a default public 2020 forest-baseline evidence layer for Digital Twin examples?
  2. How should reports communicate that GFC2020 is informative but not legally binding?
  3. What minimum evidence should be required before post-2020 disturbance is interpreted as possible EUDR-relevant deforestation?
  4. Should agricultural-conversion evidence be mandatory before a report can move beyond "disturbance detected"?
  5. How should dataset disagreement between Hansen, TMF, RADD, Sentinel confirmation, and national layers be represented?
  6. What thresholds should trigger human review?
  7. Should the Digital Twin expose "evidence gap" and "conflict register" entries as first-class report objects?
  8. Should the default evidence period use the latest available complete evidence year?
  9. Should users be allowed to pin an older evidence end year for reproducibility?
  10. How should reports communicate that the latest evidence year may lag the current calendar year?

Proposed Evidence States

  • no_2020_forest_baseline_detected
  • no_post2020_disturbance_detected
  • post2020_disturbance_detected_conversion_unknown
  • post2020_disturbance_detected_conversion_supported
  • conflicting_evidence
  • insufficient_evidence
  • human_review_required
  • temporal_coverage_gap

Acceptance Criteria

  1. Reports expose GFC2020 baseline forest area and post-2020 disturbance metrics.
  2. Disturbance is computed only for years 2021 and later.
  3. Disturbance is intersected with the 2020 forest baseline.
  4. The evidence end year is dynamically resolved from dataset availability unless explicitly pinned.
  5. Reports record the requested end year, resolved end year, per-layer latest available year, and per-layer used-through year.
  6. Agricultural-conversion evidence is represented separately.
  7. The system does not assert EUDR compliance or non-compliance.
  8. Dataset disagreement is preserved in a conflict register.
  9. Missing conversion evidence is displayed as an evidence gap.
  10. Dataset temporal-coverage gaps are displayed as evidence gaps or warnings.
  11. The output is deterministic, inspectable, and suitable for static publication in the Digital Twin portal.
  12. Private AOI-specific data is not published in public examples.
  13. Human review is triggered for non-negligible disturbance, missing conversion evidence, conflicting datasets, or temporal-coverage gaps.

Limitations

  • GFC2020 is a baseline evidence layer, not legal authority.
  • Hansen, TMF, RADD, and Sentinel layers estimate disturbance, degradation, or alerts; they do not by themselves prove agricultural conversion.
  • The latest available evidence year may lag the current calendar year.
  • Dataset versions and temporal coverage must be recorded for reproducibility.
  • National data, permits, land-use records, supply-chain evidence, and high-resolution imagery may be needed for final review.
  • The Digital Twin supports auditable evidence review and DAO governance; it does not make compliance decisions.
